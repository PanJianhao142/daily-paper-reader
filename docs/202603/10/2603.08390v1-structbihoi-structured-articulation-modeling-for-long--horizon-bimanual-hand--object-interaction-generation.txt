# 1 Introduction 

Generating human-like Hand–Object Interaction (HOI) conditioned on object geometry and language instructions is a fundamental problem for dexterous robotics [11], virtual character animation [30], and embodied AI [21]. Beyond achieving physically stable grasps, realistic manipulation must reflect task in-tent, semantic understanding, and temporally coherent motion evolution. Human manipulation is inherently goal-driven and unfolds as a continuous interaction process shaped jointly by object structure, semantic intent, and long-horizon planning. Recent advances in single-hand HOI generation [12, 24] have demon-strated promising results in grasp synthesis and manipulation planning under multimodal conditions. Leveraging large-scale motion data and generative mod-els, these approaches can produce physically plausible and semantically consis-tent motions for diverse objects. However, unlike single-hand grasping synthesis, bimanual interaction involves coordinated control of two articulated kinematic chains, where mutual constraints, contact dependencies, and asymmetric role assignment introduce significantly higher structural complexity. This results in the fact that simply extending single-hand modeling paradigms to bimanual HOI manipulation is far from trivial. Current bimanual HOI generation works can be summarized into physics-based reinforcement learning and data-driven generative modeling ways, where the former aims to learn manipulation policies in simulation considering phys-ically plausible behaviors but relying heavily on simulator fidelity and reward engineering, while the latter models interaction dynamics from large-scale mul-timodal data with sequence-based articulation architectures. Despite these sub-stantial progresses, these works often fail in long-horizon (over 150 frames) bi-manual HOI generation and suffer from three fundamental challenges: (1) Mod-eling long-range temporal dependencies becomes increasingly computationally demanding as sequence length grows, particularly in diffusion-based frameworks that repeatedly apply the sequence backbone during iterative denoising. (2) Fine-grained joint-level articulation and high-level manipulation semantics are tightly coupled, making it difficult to achieve both stable long-term planning and accurate local pose refinement. (3) Bimanual coordination requires consis-tent cross-hand dynamics, where errors in one hand may propagate temporally and spatially, leading to unstable or unrealistic motion evolution. To address these issues, we propose StructBiHOI , a Struct ured articula-tion modeling framework for long-horizon Bi manual Hand– Object Interaction generation. Our key insight is that bimanual manipulation exhibits strong tem-poral continuity and state-dependent evolution, which can be more naturally modeled through structured state-space dynamics rather than global attention. Instead of treating long-sequence generation and fine-grained pose synthesis as a monolithic problem, we introduce a hierarchical and structurally disentangled modeling strategy. Specifically, we first map multimodal conditional informa-tion—including language intent, object geometry, and temporal context—into a unified latent space. On top of this representation, we design two conditional variational autoencoders (cVAE): 1) JointVAE, which focuses on long-horizon joint motion planning and captures temporally coherent interaction dynamics; 2) ManiVAE, which operates at the single-frame level to refine detailed hand poses conditioned on the current state and semantic intent. This hierarchical decom-position separates global motion evolution from local pose refinement, reducing modeling complexity and improving stability in long sequences. To further en-able efficient long-range dependency modeling, we incorporate a Mamba-based selective state space model [5, 7] as the denoising network within a latent dif-fusion framework. The linear-complexity state-space formulation allows scalable long-horizon modeling while preserving temporal continuity. Combined with our hierarchical disentanglement strategy, the proposed framework generates glob-ally coherent and semantically consistent motion sequences for both hands and articulated object joints. We conduct extensive evaluations of StructBiHOI on the challenging bi-manual manipulation benchmark ARCTIC [6]. Experimental results demon-strate that our method consistently outperforms state-of-the-art approaches across a comprehensive set of metrics, including generation efficiency, motion realism, and coordination quality. Notably, the performance gains become increasingly significant in complex long-horizon bimanual scenarios, where precise inter-hand coordination and articulated object reasoning are required. These results high-light the effectiveness of our structured latent formulation and Mamba-based dif-fusion denoiser in modeling temporally consistent and articulation-aware inter-actions. Furthermore, we validate StructBiHOI on a single-hand grasping bench-mark to assess its generalization capability beyond bimanual settings. Despite be-ing designed for structured bimanual interaction modeling, StructBiHOI achieves superior performance compared to existing single-hand methods, demonstrating the robustness and scalability of our hierarchical generative framework. In summary, our main contributions are three-fold: 1) We present StructBi-HOI, an efficient generative framework for long-horizon bimanual hand–object interaction, designed to address the scalability and stability challenges in long-sequence HOI generation. 2) We introduce a hierarchical and structurally dis-entangled modeling strategy, decomposing long-term joint motion planning and single-frame fine-grained hand pose refinement via jointVAE and maniVAE, en-abling semantically consistent and temporally stable generation. 3) We incor-porate a Mamba-based selective state space denoiser within a latent diffusion 4 Z. # 2 Related Works 

2.1 Bimanual Hand Object Interaction 

HOI generation methods can generally be categorized into physics-based and data-driven approaches. Physics-based methods [3, 23, 28, 29] leverage reinforce-ment learning within simulation environments to learn manipulation policies that ensure physical plausibility. These approaches reduce reliance on large-scale mo-tion datasets and can produce dynamically consistent behaviors. However, their performance heavily depends on simulator fidelity and carefully engineered re-ward functions, which often limits generalization to unseen objects and interac-tion scenarios. Data-driven approaches [4, 14, 16, 19, 24, 25, 32] instead model HOI dynamics directly from large-scale multimodal motion data using generative frameworks such as autoencoder based [9, 10, 12, 17, 20] and diffusion-based models [1, 13]. By learning joint motion distributions in a data-driven manner, these meth-ods achieve realistic and semantically aligned interaction synthesis. Neverthe-less, most existing approaches focus primarily on short-horizon or single-hand settings, and often treat joint-level articulation implicitly within end-to-end se-quence models. As a result, structured modeling of long-horizon articulation dynamics and coherent bimanual coordination remains insufficiently explored. 

2.2 Long-Horizon Motion Modeling 

Recent advances in motion diffusion models have achieved remarkable success in realistic human motion synthesis. To generate full-body interactions with the environment, these models [2, 15, 16, 18, 20, 22, 26, 27, 31, 33] must simulta-neously produce coherent global body dynamics and plausible hand configura-tions. However, integrating fine-grained hand articulation with large-scale body motion significantly enlarges the state space and introduces multi-scale model-ing challenges. Several works [16, 27] adopt hierarchical or multi-stage strate-gies to decompose motion generation into coarse planning and fine refinement. Others [20] incorporate structured priors such as kinematic graphs or dynamic interaction modeling to enhance joint-level consistency. While these approaches improve full-body motion realism and grasp-oriented behaviors, they primarily focus on global motion naturalness rather than structured hand–object interac-tion modeling. In particular, articulation-level modeling of dual-hand coordina-tion and long-horizon object manipulation remains insufficiently explored within diffusion-based frameworks. # 4 Method 

4.1 Overview 

Given an object O and a motion instruction T , our goal is to generate a long-horizon bimanual hand–object interaction sequence S that captures coherent object articulation and coordinated dual-hand manipulation. To this end, we 6 Z. Wang et al. 

propose StructBiHOI , a structured articulation modeling framework that de-composes long-horizon interaction generation into hierarchical subproblems. The proposed framework consists of two components: 1) a VAE-based hierarchical articulation disentanglement module, and 2) a motion-aware sequence model for high-level interaction generation. Specifically, we introduce two conditional VAEs to disentangle global joint-level motion planning from fine-grained hand articulation refinement. After learning structured latent representations for both components, we freeze the VAE parameters and employ the motion-aware se-quence model to integrate the disentangled information and synthesize coherent long-horizon interaction trajectories. 

4.2 Articulation Disentanglement Module 

Modeling long-horizon bimanual hand–object interaction in a single latent space leads to strong entanglement between temporal planning and fine-grained artic-ulation, which in turn causes instability in long-range dependency modeling. To address this issue, we explicitly disentangle the interaction into two hierarchi-cal levels: (1) long-horizon joint-level motion evolution and (2) frame-level hand articulation refinement. Accordingly, we introduce two conditional VAEs to de-compose the original complex generation problem into structured sub-problems.

Instead of directly modeling high-dimensional hand articulation sequences, we first abstract the interaction into articulated joint trajectories, which provide a structured and lower-dimensional representation for long-horizon motion evolu-tion. # 5 Experiments 

5.1 Implementation and Metrics 

Implementation Details. We adopt a diffusion process with T = 1 ,000 timesteps and employ a cosine noise schedule. Agent-wise and frame-wise positional en-codings are incorporated during training to preserve structured temporal and Abbreviated paper title 11 

entity-level information within the denoising process. The maximum motion se-quence length is set to 150 frames. For text conditioning, we use the CLIP text encoder, while object geometry features are extracted using PointNet. We adopt a stack of 8 Mamba2 blocks as the temporal backbone of the diffusion de-noiser. Hand–object interaction visualization is implemented with PyTorch3D. Our method are trained on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU for approxi-mately four days. Notably, we partition the ARCTIC dataset into four subsets according to the number of hands involved and whether the object contains articulated joints: Bi-Art. , Bi-Rigid , Single-Art. , and Single-Rigid .

Metrics. Following prior work [13], we evaluate geometric realism, motion qual-ity, and generative diversity for bimanual HOI generation using the following metrics. Interpenetration Volume (IV) and Depth (ID). We measure ge-ometric consistency via the mean mesh intersection volume and the maximum penetration depth between hand and object across frames. capturing penetration severity relative to actual contact area. Motion Smoothness (Jerk). Temporal smoothness is evaluated by the mean jerk magnitude of hand mesh trajectories. Lower values indicate smoother and more physically plausible motion. Sam-ple Diversity (SD) and Overall Diversity (OD). We measure generative diversity via normalized pairwise ℓ2 distances between generated trajectories, computed per condition and across all samples. 

5.2 Comparisons with State-of-the-Arts 

Table 1 presents the quantitative comparison with state-of-the-art methods on bimanual HOI generation. Overall, StructBiHOI consistently achieves competi-tive or superior performance across both articulated and rigid object settings. On the Bi-Art. dataset, StructBiHOI achieves the best performance on most physical plausibility metrics. Compared with LatentHOI, our method further reduces the IV on the right hand from 0.395 to 0.382 and improves the left-hand ID from 1.319 to 1.242. Motion smoothness is also improved, where the Jerk score decreases from 0.097 to 0.092. Meanwhile, StructBiHOI maintains a reasonable object displacement (OD = 0.128), indicating physically consistent object motion during interaction. On the Bi-Rigid dataset, StructBiHOI shows consistent improvements in both interaction accuracy and motion stability. The right-hand IV is reduced from 0.085 to 0.074, and the right-hand ID decreases from 0.012 to 0.008. Our method also achieves competitive smoothness with a Jerk of 0.125 while preserving realistic object motion. These results demonstrate that the proposed structured latent modeling and hierarchical motion generation effectively improve physical plausibility, inter-action stability, and motion smoothness in long-horizon bimanual hand–object interaction synthesis.

# 6 Conclusion 

In this work, we revisit the problem of generating realistic hand–object in-teraction motions for articulated objects, where complex joint dynamics and multi-agent coordination pose significant challenges. To address these issues, we propose StructBiHOI, a structured diffusion framework for synthesizing long-horizon bimanual hand–object interaction sequences. Our key idea is to intro-duce a structured latent representation that disentangles manipulation seman-tics and object articulation while maintaining coordinated interaction dynam-ics.
